2. Deciding Particle Size

To provide some background, the camera frame rate for imaging will be at least 10Hz and could go up to 50Hz, meaning the interval between frames will be 0.02s to 0.1s. The localization is also diffraction limited (the accuracy of finding an object’s position depends on the wavelength of light used).

We want the per-frame displacement of the particle to be larger than the localization error while also being small enough to link it from frame-to-frame.

To calculate the per-frame displacement, we can use $\langle r^2 \rangle = 4Dt$.

If we replace t with Δt and square both sides of the equation, we get:

$$\Delta r = \sqrt{4D\Delta t}$$

D has to be determined separately for each particle because D depends on a, particle radius.

$$\eta = \dfrac{kT}{6\pi a D}$$

$$D = \dfrac{kT}{6\pi a \eta}$$

The confocal microscope that we are going to use has a localization error of about 5-10 nm, which is about 0.005 to 0.01 microns. All the beads are larger than this, so all the beads are permissible to use.

Data that is more than 500 frames will be inconvenient to handle, so the next step would be to determine how much total displacement each of the beads would get in 500 frames.
