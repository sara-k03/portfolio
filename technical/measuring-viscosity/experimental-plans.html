2. Deciding Particle Size

To provide some background, the camera frame rate for imaging will be at least 10Hz and could go up to 50Hz, meaning the interval between frames will be 0.02s to 0.1s. The localization is also diffraction limited, meaning that the accuracy of finding an object’s position depends on the wavelength of light used.

We want the per-frame displacement of the particle to be larger than the localization error while also being small enough to link it from frame-to-frame.

To calculate the per-frame displacement, we can use $\langle r^2 \rangle = 4Dt$.

If we replace t with Δt and square both sides of the equation, we get:

$$\Delta r = \sqrt{4D\Delta t}$$

D has to be determined separately for each particle because D depends on a, particle radius.

$$\eta = \dfrac{kT}{6\pi a D}$$

$$D = \dfrac{kT}{6\pi a \eta}$$

The table below contains the particle options that are available in the lab. I have also linked the Python script I used to determine the last two columns (the Δr columns).

https://github.com/sara-k03/viscosity-measurement

The size 0.780μm particles have the Δr values that are at the median, so it is most likely to have a per-frame displacement that is above the localization error and small enough to track across images. It is also fluorescent rather than iron, which is an advantage because iron beads are more likely to sink in a more watery medium.
