Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam

The Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam is traditionally attributed to Ādi Śaṅkarācārya in the 8th century. According to traditional accounts, when Śaṅkarācārya was 8 years old, he went to Ṛṣi Govinda Bhagavatpāda. When Bhagavatpāda asked "who are you," it's said that the Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam was Śaṅkarācārya's response.

manobuddhyahaṅkāra cittāni nāhaṁ
na ca śrotrajihve na ca ghrāṇanetre |
na ca vyoma bhūmirna tejo na vāyuḥ
cidānandarūpaḥ śivo'ham śivo'ham || 1 ||

"I am not the mind, the intellect, the ego, or the memory."

  • mano — Manas is mind, but the s becomes o before the voiced consonant b. Philosophically speaking, this is the component that processes sensory impressions.
  • buddhy — Buddhi is intellect, or the component that allows us to choose what is good and not good (and consequently make decisions). The i becomes a y before the vowel in ahaṅkāra.
  • ahaṅkāra — Ahaṅkāra is usually translated to ego, but ego in English is a bit more negative than ahaṅkāra. Ahaṅkāra is what creates self-identity.
  • cittāni — Cittāni is the storehouse of memory.
  • nāhaṁ — This is na + ahaṁ. Na means no/not and ahaṁ means I. Together, this essentially means "I am not."
  • Manas, buddhi, ahaṅkāra, and cittāni are the four parts of the human psyche in Hindu philosophy.

"Nor the ears or tongue, nor the nose or eyes."

  • na ca — Na means not and ca means and. Together, the phrase na ca means "nor."
  • śrotrajihve — Śrotra means ears. Jihvā means tongue. This is one word, and there are two things (the ears and tongue), so this invokes the dvivacana in akārānta napuṃsakaliṅga. Thus, we add an e at the end making it śrotrajihve.
  • ghrāṇanetre — Ghrāṇa means nose and netra means eyes. The e at the end is for the same reason as above.

"Nor the aether, the Earth, the fire or air."

  • na ca — Nor
  • vyoma — Sky, space, or aether.
  • bhūmirna — Bhūmiḥ + na. Bhūmiḥ is the feminine-subject-singular form of bhūmi, meaning Earth (disclaimer: I have not formally studied the declension of feminine nouns yet). Na means not (as previously said). The r is a result of the sandhi between the visarga and the voiced n that followed (when the vowel in the word is an a, the visarga gets dropped, but for any other vowel, it becomes an r).
  • tejo — Tejaḥ means fire. Tejo is a result of the sandhi between the visarga and the following voiced consonant in na, with the vowel preceding the visarga in tejaḥ being an a.
  • vāyuḥ — Air

"[I am] the form of consciousness and bliss. I am Shiva. I am Shiva."

  • The "I am" at the beginning of the line is implied because this line is not preceded by na ca like the second and third lines.
  • cidānandarūpaḥ — Cit + ānanda + rūpaḥ. Cit means consciousness. It becomes cid because the unvoiced stop t becomes voiced before a voiced sound. Ānanda means bliss, joy, or happiness. Rūpaḥ means form. Together, cidānandarūpaḥ means "the form of consciousness and bliss."
  • śivo'ham — Śivaḥ + aham. The ḥ becomes o because of sandhi (the visarga is followed by a vowel and preceded by the vowel a). Aham means I. This translates to "I am Shiva."

na ca prāṇasaṁjño na vai pañcavāyuḥ
na vā saptadhātuḥ na vā pañcakośaḥ |
na vākpāṇipādaṁ na copasthapāyu
cidānandarūpaḥ śivo'ham śivo'ham || 2 ||

"[I am] nor the vital breath, not even the five breaths."

  • na ca — nor
  • prāṇasaṁjño — Prāṇa is the vital breath of life and saṁjño is name. So this refers to that which is named breath.
  • na vai — Vai is a word of emphasis, so this phrase means even.
  • pañcavāyuḥ — Pañca means five and vāyuḥ means wind or air. So this refers to the five airs (or five breaths), which are prāṇa, apāna, udāna, samāna, and vyāna.

"Nor the seven tissues, nor the five sheaths."

  • na vā — Na is not and vā is or, so this is another way of saying nor.
  • saptadhātuḥ — Sapta is seven and dhātuḥ refers to the tissues. The seven tissues in traditional ayurveda are rasa, rakta, māṃsa, meda, asthi, majjā, and śukra.
  • pañcakośaḥ — Pañca means five. Kośaḥ means sheaths. The five sheaths are the five layers that encase the soul: annamaya kośa (the physical sheath), prāṇamaya kośa (the vital energy/breath sheath), manomaya kośa (the mental/sensory sheath), vijñānamaya kośa (the wisdom/intellect sheath), and the ānandamaya kośa (the bliss sheath).

"Not the voice, hand, or foot, nor the reproductive or excretory organs."

  • na — not
  • vākpāṇipādaṁ — Vāk is voice, pāṇi is hand, pādaṁ is foot.
  • copasthapāyu — Ca + upastha + pāyu. The sandhi between ca and upastha creates copastha. Ca is and, upastha refers to the reproductive organs, and pāyu refers to the excretory organ.

"[I am] the form of consciousness and bliss. I am Shiva. I am Shiva."

  • Same as the last line of the first stanza.

na me dveṣarāgau na me lobhamohau
mado naiva me naiva mātsaryabhāvaḥ |
na dharmo na cārtho na kāmo na mokṣaḥ
cidānandarūpaḥ śivo'ham śivo'ham || 3 ||

"Not in me are hatred and attachment, not in me are greed and delusion."

  • na — not
  • me — of me, another way to say mama
  • dveṣarāgau — Dveṣa means hatred. Rāga means attachment. Dveṣarāgau is the dual-noun form of these two things (-au is dvivacana).
  • lobhamohau — dual-noun form of lobha (meaning greed) and moha (meaning delusion).

"Not even in me are arrogance and jealousy."

  • mado — This is actually madaḥ (arrogance), but became mado because of sandi with the next letter n.
  • naiva — na + eva, meaning not even
  • me — in me
  • mātsaryabhāvaḥ — Mātsarya is jealousy. Bhāvaḥ means the state. This means the state of jealousy.

"Not dharma nor wealth, nor pleasure or moksha."

  • na — not
  • dharmo — Dharma became dharmo because of sandhi. Dharma is not a word worth translating but would best be translated to "righteous duty."
  • cārtho — Ca + artha (with sandhi). Artha translates to wealth.
  • kāmo — Kāma with sandhi. Kāma means pleasure.
  • mokṣaḥ — Mokṣaḥ is also not worth translating, but it refers to the liberation from reincarnation.
  • Dharma, kāma, artha, and mokṣa are the puruṣārthaḥ, or the four goals of life.

"[I am] the form of consciousness and bliss. I am Shiva. I am Shiva."

  • Same as the last line of the first stanza.

na puṇyaṁ na pāpaṁ na saukhyaṁ na duḥkhaṁ
na mantro na tīrthaṁ na vedā na yajñāḥ |
ahaṁ bhojanaṁ naiva bhojyaṁ na bhoktā
cidānandarūpaḥ śivo'ham śivo'ham || 4 ||

"Not virtue, not sin, not happiness, not sadness"

  • na — not
  • puṇyaṁ — virtue
  • pāpaṁ — sin
  • saukhyaṁ — happiness
  • duḥkhaṁ — sadness

"Not mantras, not a pilgrimage site, not scripture, not rituals"

  • na — not
  • mantro — mantraḥ with sandhi
  • tīrthaṁ — this does not refer to the water at the temple, but a holy a place/pilgrimage site
  • vedā — the vedas, or other sacred texts
  • yajñāḥ — this usually refers to yagnas specifically, but can be interpreted as a blanket term for all rituals

"I am not the enjoyment of the experience, the object of the experience being enjoyed, nor the enjoyer of an experience."

  • ahaṁ — I
  • bhojanaṁ — This generally means food, but in the context of this philosophical piece, it means the act of enjoying experience.
  • naiva — na + eva, not even
  • bhojyaṁ — the object of the act of enjoying an experience
  • na — not
  • bhoktā — the enjoyer of an experience

"[I am] the form of consciousness and bliss. I am Shiva. I am Shiva."

  • Same as the last line of the first stanza.

na mṛtyurna śaṅkā na me jātibhedaḥ
pitā naiva me naiva mātā na janmaḥ |
na bandhurna mitraṁ gururnaiva śiṣyaṁ
cidānandarūpaḥ śivo'ham śivo'ham || 5 ||

"Not death, not fear, not the distinction of my lineage."

  • na — not
  • mṛtyurna — mṛtyuḥ + na becomes mṛtyurna because of sandhi. mṛtyuḥ means death.
  • śaṅkā — fear or doubt
  • me — of me
  • jātibhedaḥ — jāti + bhedaḥ. jāti means lineage or caste and bhedaḥ means distinction/differenciation. Together this means the distinction of lineages.

"I have no father, no mother, and not even birth."

  • pitā — father
  • naiva — not even
  • me — of me (this implies the "I have")
  • mātā — mother
  • na — not
  • janmaḥ — birth

"Not a relative, friend, teacher or even disciple."

  • na — no
  • bandhurna — bandhuḥ + na becomes bandhurna through sandhi. bandhuḥ means relative.
  • mitram — friend
  • guru — teacher
  • naiva — not even
  • śiṣyaṁ — student

"[I am] the form of consciousness and bliss. I am Shiva. I am Shiva."

  • Same as the last line of the first stanza.

ahaṁ nirvikalpo nirākārarūpo
vibhutvācca sarvatra sarvendriyāṇām |
na cāsaṅgataṁ naiva muktirna meyaḥ
cidānandarūpaḥ śivo'ham śivo'ham || 6 ||

"I am without conceptual distinction and without form."

  • aham — I
  • nirvikalpo — nir + vikalpo. nir means without and vikalpo means conceptual distinction (such as you or I, student or teacher, etc.).
  • nirākārarūpaḥ — nir + ākāra + rūpaḥ. nir means without, ākāra and rūpaḥ both mean form. Combined this word means without form.

"[I am] from pervasiveness everywhere and of all senses."

  • vibhutvācca — vibhutvāt + ca becomes vibhutvācca. vibhu means pervasive, tva implies "ness," and this word is in the pañcamī vibhakti form. Put together, this means "from pervasiveness."
  • sarvatra — everywhere
  • sarvendriyāṇām — sarva + indriyāṇām. indriyā means sense. indriyāṇām is bahuvacana ṣaṣṭhī vibhakti, so together this means, "of all senses."

"Not attachment, nor freedom, nor capable of being known."

  • na — not
  • cāsaṅgataṁ — ca + asaṅgataṁ. asaṅgataṁ means unattachment.
  • naiva — not even
  • muktirna — mukti + na becomes muktirna through sandhi. mukti means liberation.
  • meyaḥ — discernable/capable of being known

"[I am] the form of consciousness and bliss. I am Shiva. I am Shiva."

  • Same as the last line of the first stanza.
