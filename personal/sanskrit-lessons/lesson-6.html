Bhagavad Gītā 6.30

yo māṁ paśyati sarvatra sarvaṁ ca mayi paśyati |
tasyāhaṁ na praṇaśyāmi sa ca me na praṇaśyati ||

yo - Same as yaḥ but changed with sandhi; yaḥ means "he who" or "one who"
māṁ - The word "me" when "me" is the object
paśyati - sees
sarvatra - sarva + atra; literally means "every here" which we can deduce to mean everywhere
sarvam - Means "all" but when it is the object of the sentence
ca - and
mayi - In me
paśyati - sees

Combined, the first line becomes:

He who - me - sees - everywhere - all - and - in me - sees → He who sees me everywhere and sees all in me.

tasya - "To him" or "for him"
aham - I
na - not
praṇaśyāmi - disappear, perish, am lost
saḥ - he
ca - and
me - to me
na - not
praṇaśyati - disappears, perishes, is lost

Combined, the second line becomes:

To him - I - not - disappear - he - and - to me - not - disappears → I am never lost to him, and he is never lost to me.

So the meaning of the entire verse is:

He who sees me everywhere and sees all in me, to him I am never lost, and he is never lost to me.
