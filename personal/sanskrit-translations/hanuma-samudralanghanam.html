Hanuma Samudralaṅghanam

This scene depicting Hanuman's leap to Lanka contains verses 8 to 12 of Sarga 1 (Chapter 1) of the Sundara Kanda in the Valmiki Ramayana. Verse numberings may differ depending on version.

añjaliṃ prāṅmukhaḥ kurvanpavanāyātmayonaye |
tato hi vavṛdhe gantuṃ dakṣiṇo dakṣiṇāṃ diśam || 8 ||

Facing east, making salutations to the self-originated Pavana, thereupon, he indeed increased effort to go south.

  • añjaliṃ — Añjali means folded hands in reverence or divine offering. Añjaliṃ means that añjali is the object of an action.
  • prāṅmukhaḥ — This is a combination of prāk + mukhaḥ. When a final k, t, ṭ, or p comes before n or m, it switches to the nasal sound in its varga. So here, prāk becomes prāṅ. In terms of meaning, prāk means east, in front of, or formerly. In this context, it means east. Mukhaḥ means face. So, prāṅmukhaḥ means east-facing.
  • kurvan — This comes from the root kṛ which means to make/to do (like karna in Hindi). Kurvan is the singular masculine present participle form (-ing form in English) of kṛ, so it translates to "doing."
  • pavanāyātmayonaye — This is pavanāya + ātmayonaye. Pavana is another name for Vayu. Pavanāya means is the akārānta puṃliṅga meaning "for Pavana." Ātmayonaye comes from ātmayoni, which means self-originated. Ātmayonaye is ikārānta puṃliṅga noun form meaning "for atmayoni."
  • Put together, we get "Folded hands - facing east - making (masculine) - for the self-originated Pavana." Structuring this as an actual sentence (and adding in some cultural context), we get the first line: "Facing east, making salutations to the self-originated Pavana,".
  • tato — An avyayāḥ (indeclinable) meaning thereupon.
  • hi — Another avyayāḥ added for emphasis meaning indeed/only.
  • vavṛdhe — This comes from the root vṛdh meaning to grow or increase effort. Vavṛdhe is the perfect past tense form (liṭ lakāra) of this.
  • gantuṃ — The infinitive form of the root gam, which means to go.
  • dakṣiṇo dakṣiṇāṃ — Dakṣiṇo comes from dakṣiṇaḥ which is the nominative singular masculine form of the word meaning south. The -o at the end comes from the sandhi with dakṣiṇāṃ. Dakṣiṇāṃ is the singular feminine accusative form of the word meaning south. Dakṣiṇo is meant to describe that Hanuman is facing south, which is why the word is masculine. Dakṣiṇāṃ is feminine and accusative in order to agree with the next word, diśam.
  • diśam — Diś means direction and it is in the accusative form because it is the object of the action of going. It is not in the locative form because it's describing a final destination, not where something is located.
  • Putting together the second part of the verse gives us, "thereupon - indeed - he increased effort - to go - south (describing Hanuman) - to the southern direction." Structuring this as an actual sentence we get, "thereupon, he indeed increased effort to go south."
