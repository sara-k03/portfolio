Shankar Exercise 13.1.1

$$\frac{d^2 v}{d\rho^2} - 2\frac{dv}{d\rho} + \left[\frac{e^2\lambda}{\rho} - \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\rho^2}\right] v = 0 \tag{13.1.8}$$ $$\lambda = \left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2 W}\right)^{1/2} \tag{13.1.9}$$ $$V_{E\ell} = \rho^{\ell+1} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \rho^k \tag{13.1.10}$$ $$\frac{C_{k+1}}{C_k} = \frac{-e^2\lambda + 2(k+\ell+1)}{(k+\ell+2)(k+\ell+1) - \ell(\ell+1)} \tag{13.1.11}$$ $$\mathcal{E} = -W = \frac{-me^4}{2\hbar^2 (k+\ell+1)^2}, \qquad k = 0,1,2,\dots,\ \ell = 0,1,2,\dots \tag{13.1.14}$$

Derivation of 13.1.11

The reason 13.1.10 is a power-series is because the textbook employs the power-series method to solve the differential equation. This uses the idea that instead of guessing the entire function, you can guess that it can be written as an infinite polynomial and use the ODE to guess the coefficients.

13.1.11 is the recurrence relationship, which is what we want to determine. To do this, we have to re-write the series so it becomes more easily differentiable, differentiate the series to be able to substitute terms into the ODE, and then re-organize to obtain the recurrence relation.

Rewriting the series:

$$V_{E\ell} = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \rho^{k+\ell+1}$$

Taking the derivatives to plug into the ODE:

$$ \begin{aligned} v' &= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k (k+\ell+1)\, \rho^{k+\ell} \\ v'' &= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k (k+\ell+1)(k+\ell)\, \rho^{k+\ell-1} \end{aligned} $$

Substituting into the differential equation

$$v'' - 2v' + \left[\frac{e^2\lambda}{\rho} - \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\rho^2}\right] v = 0$$ $$ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k (k+\ell+1)(k+\ell)\, \rho^{k+\ell-1} - 2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k (k+\ell+1)\, \rho^{k+\ell} + \frac{e^2\lambda}{\rho} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \rho^{k+\ell+1} - \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\rho^2} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \rho^{k+\ell+1} = 0 $$

which simplifies to

$$ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k (k+\ell+1)(k+\ell)\, \rho^{k+\ell-1} - 2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k (k+\ell+1)\, \rho^{k+\ell} + e^2\lambda \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \rho^{k+\ell} - \ell(\ell+1) \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \rho^{k+\ell-1} = 0 $$

Combining the terms:

$$ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \big[(k+\ell+1)(k+\ell) - \ell(\ell+1)\big]\, \rho^{k+\ell-1} + \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} C_k \big[e^2\lambda - 2(k+\ell+1)\big]\, \rho^{k+\ell} = 0 $$

To further combine these terms, the first term needs to be re-indexed.

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} C_{k+1} \big[(k+\ell+2)(k+\ell+1) - \ell(\ell+1)\big]\, \rho^{k+\ell}$$

So for \(k \ge 1\):

$$ C_{k+1} \big[(k+\ell+2)(k+\ell+1) - \ell(\ell+1)\big]\, \rho^{k+\ell} = -C_k \big[e^2\lambda - 2(k+\ell+1)\big]\, \rho^{k+\ell} $$

Solving for \(C_{k+1}/C_k\) gives us Eq. 13.1.11:

$$\boxed{\dfrac{C_{k+1}}{C_k} = \dfrac{-e^2\lambda + 2(k+\ell+1)}{(k+\ell+2)(k+\ell+1) - \ell(\ell+1)}}$$

Deriving Eq. 13.1.14

Use Eq. 13.1.9 and solve for \(W\), and negate it to derive Eq. 13.1.14.

$$ \begin{aligned} \lambda &= \sqrt{\frac{2m}{\hbar^2 W}} \ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda^2 = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2 W} \\ W &= \frac{2m}{\hbar^2 \lambda^2} \end{aligned} $$

The series \(V_{E\ell}\) ultimately contributes to a wavefunction, so it has to terminate at some point, otherwise the wavefunction will blow up and cannot represent a physical electron.

For the series to terminate, \(C_{k+1}\) eventually needs to be \(0\).

$$-e^2\lambda + 2(k+\ell+1) = 0$$

Substituting 13.1.9 for \(\lambda\):

$$e^2 \sqrt{\frac{2m}{\hbar^2 W}} = 2(k+\ell+1)$$

\(\mathcal{E} = -W\), so we need to find the values of \(-W\) that satisfy this condition (thus creating the quantized energy levels).

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{2e^4 m}{\hbar^2 W} &= 4(k+\ell+1)^2 \\ W &= \frac{e^4 m}{2\hbar^2 (k+\ell+1)^2} \end{aligned} $$

Negating this gives us Eq. 13.1.14.

$$\boxed{\mathcal{E} = -W = \dfrac{-me^4}{2\hbar^2 (k+\ell+1)^2}}$$
