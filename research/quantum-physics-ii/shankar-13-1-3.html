Shankar Exercise 13.1.3

Problem: Starting from the recursion relation, obtain \(\psi_{210}\).
$$\psi_{210} = \left(\frac{1}{32\pi a_0^3}\right)^{1/2} \frac{r}{a_0} e^{-r/2a_0} \cos\theta$$

Recursion relation:

$$\frac{C_{k+1}}{C_k} = \frac{-e^2\lambda + 2(k+\ell+1)}{(k+\ell+2)(k+\ell+1) - \ell(\ell+1)}$$

\(\psi_{210}\) means \(n=2\), \(\ell=1\), and \(m=0\). The problem says to use the recursion relation.

Equation 13.1.15 says \(n = k + \ell + 1\). Since \(n=2\) and \(\ell=1\), so \(k=0\). Therefore, the series terminates at \(k=0\).

$$v_{E\ell} = \rho^{\ell+1} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k \rho^k$$ $$v = C_0 \rho^2$$

\(v\) is not the full radial wavefunction.

Eq (13.1.7):

$$U_{E\ell} = e^{-\rho} V_{E\ell}$$

Eq (13.1.3):

$$\psi_{n\ell m}(r,\theta,\phi) = R_{E\ell}(r)\, Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi) = \frac{U_{E\ell}}{r} Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi)$$

So

$$R_{E\ell} = \frac{U_{E\ell}}{r} = \frac{1}{r} \cdot e^{-\rho} V_{E\ell}$$ $$R_{21} = \frac{e^{-\rho}}{r} \cdot C_0 \rho^2$$

The \(e\) term in the \(\psi_{210}\) form given in the textbook does not contain a \(\rho\), implying that we have to find \(\rho\).

Eq (13.1.6):

$$\rho = r\sqrt{\frac{2mW}{\hbar^2}}$$

From question 13.1.1, we found:

$$W = \frac{me^4}{2\hbar^2(k+\ell+1)^2} = \frac{me^4}{2\hbar^2 n^2}$$

So,

$$\rho = r\sqrt{\frac{2m}{\hbar^2} \cdot \frac{me^4}{2\hbar^2 n^2}} = r\sqrt{\frac{m^2 e^4}{\hbar^4 n^2}}$$ $$\rho = \frac{me^2 r}{\hbar^2 n}$$

\(\psi_{210}\) has the Bohr radius \(a_0 = \dfrac{\hbar^2}{me^2}\) in it, so

$$\rho = \frac{r}{a_0 n} = \frac{r}{2a_0}$$

So,

$$ R_{21} = \frac{C_0}{r}\left(\frac{r}{2a_0}\right)^2 e^{-r/2a_0} = \frac{C_0}{4a_0} \cdot \frac{r}{a_0}\, e^{-r/2a_0} $$

To achieve the form in the textbook, we can treat \(\frac{C_0}{4a_0}\) as a constant that gets absorbed into the normalization constant later.

$$R_{21} \propto \frac{r}{a_0} e^{-r/2a_0}$$

The spherical harmonic associated with \(m=0\) and \(\ell=1\) is

$$Y_1^0 = \sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}} \cos\theta$$

\(\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4\pi}}\) is also just a constant, so \(Y_1^0 \propto \cos\theta\).

Now we know:

$$\psi_{210} \propto \frac{r}{a_0} e^{-r/2a_0} \cos\theta$$

Since \(\psi_{210}\) still needs to get normalized:

$$\psi_{210} = A\, \frac{r}{a_0} e^{-r/2a_0} \cos\theta$$

Solving for the normalization constant yields \(A = \left(\frac{1}{32\pi a_0^3}\right)^{1/2}\), which gives us the full form of the wavefunction given in the textbook.

$$\psi_{210} = \left(\frac{1}{32\pi a_0^3}\right)^{1/2} \frac{r}{a_0} e^{-r/2a_0} \cos\theta$$
